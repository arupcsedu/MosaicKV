\begin{abstract}
Multimodal large language models retain visual, OCR, layout, and text states
throughout decoding, making the key--value (KV) cache a capacity bottleneck.
MosaicKV is a training-free framework that forecasts future query demand,
organizes cross-modal evidence, and selects exact KV blocks under a physical
byte budget.  MosaicKV defines an extensible three-tier cache, but this paper
evaluates only its conservative exact-selection path; prototype merging and
residual repair are specified but inactive.  On 9,000 paired LLaVA-1.5-7B
examples, the selected operating point uses approximately 43.5\% less active
KV memory than Prompt Top-K while retaining 98.99--100\% of FullKV task
quality.  To separate selector quality from operating-point choice, we also
sweep 16, 22, 30, 40, and 60 MiB on 256 MMStar, TextVQA, and DocVQA examples
per task.  At exactly matched per-sample bytes, MosaicKV matches or exceeds
Prompt Top-K at 13 of 15 points, with statistically resolved gains at low
TextVQA and DocVQA budgets.  The observed HF-eager decode reduction vanishes
under vLLM, and online planning dominates end-to-end latency.  We therefore
claim a low-budget quality--memory improvement, not serving acceleration or
an empirical benefit from the inactive tiers.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Results and claim boundary.}
Our original 9,000-example LLaVA-1.5 operating point preserves
98.99--100\% of FullKV task quality while using approximately 43.5\% fewer
active KV bytes than Prompt Top-K.  Because the two methods originally ran at
different byte budgets, we add an exactly byte-matched five-cap sweep.  On
256 examples per task, MosaicKV matches or improves Prompt Top-K at 13 of 15
points; its clearest gains occur at low TextVQA and DocVQA budgets.  This
supports the selection policy in the measured low-memory regime, while the
two higher-memory reversals prevent a universal dominance claim.  Separate
published-baseline diagnostics expose unresolved PrefixKV
checkpoint/profile mismatch and are not used in the headline comparison.
Finally, the HF-eager decode reduction does not transfer to vLLM and is
overwhelmed by planning cost, so we make no end-to-end serving claim.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a future-conditioned formulation of multimodal KV selection that
  combines query forecasting, sparse evidence relations, and value-sensitive
  utility under an explicit physical-byte budget;
  \item a compatibility-gated exact-selection implementation that preserves
  the tensors and logical positions of retained blocks, together with an
  extensible but currently unevaluated prototype/residual interface; and
  \item an exactly byte-matched quality--memory study that separates selector
  quality from operating-point choice and reports negative baseline-fidelity,
  backend-transfer, peak-memory, and end-to-end-latency results.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Validated Path and Extensible Cache Tiers}
\label{sec:validated-design-scope}

MosaicKV's validated path starts from the full multimodal cache, predicts
which evidence future decoder queries may need, constructs sparse relations
among compatible evidence blocks, and chooses exact blocks under a hard byte
cap.  For selected set \(S_{\mathrm{exact}}\), the active cache used in this
paper is
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde S_{\mathrm{KV}}=S_{\mathrm{exact}},\qquad
  \operatorname{bytes}(S_{\mathrm{exact}})\le B.
\end{equation}
Every retained K/V tensor is copied or aliased without averaging,
quantization, or position renumbering.  This preserves the numerical state of
\emph{kept} blocks; it does not preserve evidence assigned to evicted blocks.

The broader architecture admits
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde S_{\mathrm{KV}}=
  (S_{\mathrm{exact}},S_{\mathrm{proto}},S_{\mathrm{residual}}),
\end{equation}
where compatible source blocks could be summarized as prototypes and retained
outside the active device cache for repair.  These operations are guarded by
layer, KV-head, modality, logical-span, and positional-encoding compatibility.
In the reported LLaVA campaign, the gate rejected the prototype assignments;
residual storage and repair consequently remained inactive.  We specify these
interfaces for extensibility but do not attribute any measured result to
them.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the byte-matched experiment establishes.}
The 16--60 MiB sweep removes the operating-point confound for Prompt Top-K:
within every pair, active KV storage, blocks, mandatory policy, model, input,
and decoding controls are identical.  MosaicKV's advantage at low TextVQA and
DocVQA budgets therefore reflects which blocks are retained rather than how
many bytes are allocated.  MMStar is flat, and Prompt Top-K has higher point
estimates at 60 MiB on TextVQA and DocVQA, so the result is a regime-specific
improvement rather than universal selector superiority.

\paragraph{Published baselines.}
The PrefixKV diagnostic does not reproduce the paper's near-lossless behavior
and must not be presented as evidence against PrefixKV.  Its legacy
checkpoint, patched Transformers runtime, incompletely documented profile
calibration, and different cache-budget definition are material differences
from the common HF run.  We retain the negative rows, explain the mismatch,
and remove PrefixKV from the primary ranking.  VL-Cache-Reimpl shows the
expected improvement as retention grows on its paper model family and reaches
FullKV TextVQA score at 40\%, but it is not an exact published-table
reproduction.  LOOK-M-Reimpl is treated similarly.  The primary comparative
claim is restricted to the exactly controlled Prompt Top-K baseline.

\paragraph{Systems scope.}
HF eager attention performs sequence-length-dependent score, mask, and
softmax work, making its 5.19--5.89\% decode reduction plausible even when the
saved KV payload is small relative to model weights.  vLLM's paged attention
shows only \(-0.21\) to \(-0.09\)\% change in the same short-context regime,
which is the relevant evidence for an optimized serving engine.  Since online
planning also dominates end-to-end time and temporary tensors raise peak
allocation, MosaicKV is presently a capacity/quality method rather than a
latency optimization.  Long multimodal contexts, batching, and a fused
planner require separate measurement.

\paragraph{Generality and statistical scope.}
The central result uses one LLaVA model family.  The byte-matched identities
come from the earlier campaign, so they validate the plotted selector curve
but are not an independent held-out replication.  Confidence intervals are
paired but uncorrected for 15 comparisons.  A paper-level architecture claim
requires preregistered held-out tests on additional model families.

\section{Conclusion}

MosaicKV combines future-query forecasting, cross-modal evidence modeling,
and value-aware exact-block selection under a measured byte budget.  On
LLaVA-1.5-7B, its low-memory operating point retains near-FullKV task quality,
and an exactly byte-matched sweep shows that its advantage over Prompt Top-K
persists across the 16--40 MiB regime on MMStar, TextVQA, and DocVQA.  The
result is strongest for OCR and document questions; it does not extend to
every 60 MiB point.  Published-method reimplementations are reported only as
fidelity diagnostics, and the current experiments do not evaluate prototype
merging or residual repair.  Because the HF-eager decode observation
disappears under vLLM and planning dominates total time, our supported claim
is improved low-budget quality per active KV byte, not end-to-end serving
acceleration.
