The key--value (KV) cache of a vision--language model stores two projections
of a common attention input. We study EviCastKV-S, a training-free storage
variant of EviCastKV that retains a quantized shared source, protects selected
KV regions exactly, and reconstructs layer-local attention operands using
the unchanged model weights. Generated history uses a lossless source-based
representation with numerical corrections. Request-private reconstruction
buffers and eligible eager-attention replay reduce persistent storage without
requiring a new serving engine. On 256 COCO images with LLaVA-1.5-7B,
EviCastKV-S uses \EviInitial~MiB initially, \EviFinal~MiB finally, and
\EviPeak~MiB mean audited decode-owned peak storage, and scores
\EviCider~CIDEr. Its measured operating point is smaller and faster than
four local compressed adaptations, but has lower point-estimate quality than
VL-Cache and LOOK-M adaptations. Paired intervals do not establish quality
preservation against those methods. We distinguish operating-point evidence
from byte-matched superiority, decode-owned peaks from whole-request memory,
and the evaluated storage path from inactive forecasting, graph-selection,
prototype and repair mechanisms. The result is an auditable account of the
benefits and remaining limits of shared-source multimodal cache storage.
