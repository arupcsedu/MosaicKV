\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{KV selection and allocation.}
H2O retains heavy hitters and recency; StreamingLLM emphasizes attention sinks;
SnapKV uses an observation window; PyramidKV adapts layer capacity
\citep{zhang2023h2o,xiao2024streaming,li2024snapkv,cai2024pyramidkv}.
They establish that importance, protected context, and allocation granularity
matter. MosaicKV combines evidence relationships with routed execution and a
physical capacity transaction; attention-based selection itself is not novel.

\paragraph{Multimodal compression.}
LOOK-M uses text-prior selection and compensatory merging; PrefixKV searches
layer-specific importance-prefix configurations; VL-Cache uses modality-aware
attention and sparsity-driven allocation
\citep{wan2024lookm,wang2025prefixkv,tu2025vlcache}. We distinguish native
conventions from common-byte adaptations. Attention-output matching also has
prior work \citep{attentionmatching2026}; our development diagnostics do not
claim that reconstructing attention output guarantees answer preservation.

\paragraph{Execution and evaluation.}
FlashAttention improves attention IO; PagedAttention addresses allocation and
sharing \citep{dao2022flash,dao2023flash2,kwon2023paged}. Both can complement
semantic eviction but change bottlenecks. CIDEr, ANLS, ROUGE-L, and Accuracy
measure distinct properties
\citep{vedantam2015cider,mathew2021docvqa,lin2004rouge,song2024milebench}.
Our analysis uses native reducers and paired identities rather than a
cross-metric average.
